The fine print, up front

How the math works

Four things to know. No sleight of hand.

  1. 1

    Multiply the long shots. Stoner asked his students: for each prophecy, what are the odds one random person matches it? Bethlehem birth: 1 in 280,000. Preceded by a messenger: 1 in 1,000. And so on. Multiply all eight — first-week statistics — and one person faces about 1 in 10²⁸. Then Stoner steel-manned the skeptic: divide by the ~88 billion people who've lived since the prophecies were written ("with that many people, someone was bound to match"). Leftover: 1 in 10¹⁷. Picture Texas two feet deep in silver dollars, one marked, found blindfolded. The arithmetic checks out.

  2. 2

    The numbers are judgment calls. Seven of the eight are educated guesses, not measurements — Stoner says so himself. Three times his students argued for bigger odds and he deliberately picked smaller ones. Disagree with an estimate? Good. That's exactly what the doubt dial is for.

  3. 3

    Independence is the weak link. Multiplying only works if each prophecy is independent. Three aren't — the betrayal, the thirty coins, and the potter's field are one connected story (Zechariah 11:12–13). We're telling you before the critics do. Merge them into a single event in the deep-dives, or just uncheck them.

  4. 4

    Surprising isn't the same as proven. The number measures how shocking a chance match would be — not what you should conclude. Stoner picked these 8 from 300+ candidates. Some fulfillments may have been arranged — the Gospels say Jesus set up the donkey himself. And no equation can tell you whether the Gospels report history or shape it. That's your call.

“If the reader does not agree with the estimates given, he may make his own estimates and then carry them through to their logical conclusions.”
— Peter W. Stoner, Science Speaks. That's exactly what the controls on this page do. Weaken every assumption, uncheck the ones you don't buy, and watch the total. If the case can't survive your skepticism, it doesn't deserve your belief — and if it can, that's worth knowing too.

The punchline

Eight was just the warm-up.

Everything you just ran was eight prophecies. Eight.

Stoner didn't stop there either. He extended the same math to 48 prophecies and landed on 1 in 10157 — a one followed by 157 zeros. His illustration: a solid ball of electrons stretching across the universe. Mark one. Find it blindfolded.

And the catalogs keep going. Lists of Old Testament passages the New Testament applies to Jesus run past 300 — some top 365. Not every one is equally strong; we'll say that before anyone else does. Some are dependent. Some may have been arranged. Some are weakly supported. List size alone proves nothing. But the bar is clear: each additional specific, independent, well-supported prophecy with a defensible estimate pushes the number further.

We gave you eight. We handed you the doubt dial. We told you every weakness first. The math survived. Now imagine 300 — done right.
