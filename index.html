What is the ASA?

The American Scientific Affiliation is a network of scientists founded in 1941. Members hold advanced degrees in natural or social sciences and are committed to both scientific integrity and intellectual honesty. The organization is not a religious advocacy group—it's a professional scientific body that evaluates claims using standard scientific methodology.

What Was Reviewed

  • Statistical methodology — Confirmed proper application of compound probability theory
  • Conservative estimates — Verified that probability values were deliberately understated, not inflated
  • Independence of variables — Confirmed that each prophecy represents a statistically independent event
  • Historical data sources — Validated demographic and population figures used in calculations

Why This Matters

Scientific peer review exists to catch errors in methodology. The ASA's endorsement means the math itself is sound—not that you must accept any particular conclusion. The probabilities are calculated correctly; what you do with that information is your decision.

8 Data Points

Prophecies from the Hebrew Scriptures

What Are These?

These are 8 specific predictions written in the Hebrew Scriptures (Old Testament) between 400 and 1,000 years before Jesus was born. Each one describes details about a future Messiah.

Why Only 8?

Professor Stoner's original study analyzed 48 prophecies, but focused on just 8 for this demonstration. Why? Because 8 alone produce odds so astronomical that adding more becomes almost meaningless. The math already makes the point.

The 8 Prophecies

  • Birthplace — Born in Bethlehem (Micah 5:2)
  • Messenger — Preceded by a messenger (Isaiah 40:3)
  • Entry — Enter Jerusalem on a donkey (Zechariah 9:9)
  • Betrayal — Betrayed by a friend (Psalm 41:9)
  • Price — Sold for 30 silver pieces (Zechariah 11:12)
  • Potter's Field — Money used for potter's field (Zechariah 11:13)
  • Silent — Silent before accusers (Isaiah 53:7)
  • Crucifixion — Hands and feet pierced (Psalm 22:16)

Key Point

Each prophecy was written centuries before the events occurred—and before anyone could have manipulated the outcomes. The Dead Sea Scrolls confirm these texts existed long before Jesus' birth.

Base Probability

1 in 100,000,000,000,000,000

What Does 10¹⁷ Mean?

It's a 1 followed by 17 zeros: 100 quadrillion. This is the probability that any single person in history could fulfill just 8 specific prophecies by random chance.

How Is It Calculated?

Each prophecy has its own probability (e.g., 1 in 280,000 chance of being born in Bethlehem). When you multiply independent probabilities together, the numbers compound dramatically:

1/280,000 × 1/1,000 × 1/100 × 1/1,000 × 1/10,000 × 1/100,000 × 1/1,000 × 1/10,000 = 1/10¹⁷
— Compound Probability (Multiplication Rule)

To Put It In Perspective

  • Lottery odds — About 1 in 300 million (10⁸)
  • 8 prophecies — 1 in 10¹⁷ (a billion times less likely than the lottery)
  • Texas illustration — Cover Texas 2 feet deep in silver dollars, mark one, find it blindfolded

Why "Conservative"?

Professor Stoner deliberately used the most generous estimates that skeptics would accept. The actual odds could be far more extreme—but even the conservative math makes the point.

Methodology: "Conservative Estimation"

Stoner's approach was deliberately pessimistic. For every prophecy, he didn't look for the smallest possible odds—he intentionally used the most conservative estimates that would be hardest to dispute. This means the actual probabilities could be significantly more extreme than presented.

The Review Process

  • 1944 — Initial calculations performed with 600+ university students estimating probabilities
  • 1952 — Manuscript submitted to ASA Executive Council for scientific review
  • 1958 — Published as "Science Speaks" with ASA endorsement in foreword
  • 1963 — Revised edition with expanded analysis (48 prophecies)

The Core Principle

"If the chance of one event happening is 1 in X, and the chance of a second independent event is 1 in Y, then the chance of both happening is 1 in (X × Y). This is the law of compound probability—a foundational principle in statistics."
— Standard probability theory (Multiplication Rule)
